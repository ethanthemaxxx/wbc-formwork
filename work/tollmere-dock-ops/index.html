Case

Tollmere dock, from scan to dispatch.

A regional freight forwarder. Two docks, three shifts, one paper exception log.

Back to work

Client: Tollmere Freight

Sector: Freight forwarding

Year: 2026

Length: 9 weeks

TOLLMERE

01 Context

Tollmere moves pallets between two docks and 40 carriers. Every inbound scan that did not match its booking became an exception.

Leads walked the floor with a clipboard. Exceptions were logged on paper, retyped at the end of the shift and read the next morning. A short count at 06:00 reached the customer at 15:00. Tollmere asked us for an assessment, not a tool.

02 Process map

We shadowed three shifts. We drew eleven steps. Four of them carried all the delay.

As-is: 6 steps, 1 day. To-be: 5 steps, 12 minutes. Illustrative.

Steps three to five were pure transport. Information moved by hand from a clipboard to a screen to an inbox. We removed the transport and kept the decision.

03 What we built

Two agents, one rule set and a live board. Nothing else.

  • Intake agent

    Reads each scanner event, compares it with the booking and tags the mismatch: short, over, damaged or wrong lane.

  • Dispatch rules

    Routes each exception to the lead on shift. Rules are plain text. Dispatch edits them without us.

  • Floor board

    One screen per dock lists open exceptions, age and owner. Leads close them from the handheld.

  • As-built file

    Steps, rules, owners and failure paths in one document. Tollmere’s own team runs it.

The agent proposes. The lead decides. Any exception tagged damaged waits for a person before a carrier is contacted.

05 What’s next

Tollmere is extending the same flow to its second dock and to inbound customs documents.

Next on the plan: a carrier-facing status message drafted from each alert, and a monthly report of exception causes by carrier. Both reuse the rules already in place.
